02
The problem — in Attestcoin's own words

“Billions lost to bridge exploits, again and again.”

July 2023: Multichain ran wrapped assets across ten chains against custodied reserves, held roughly $1.26B, and lost about $126–130M when centralised control failed. Every chain could have shown a healthy reserve check while the system as a whole was insolvent — because no per-chain design ever looks at the other chains.

A year earlier, Cashio holders found their tokens were backed by nothing and $52.8M evaporated. In neither case was there a moment at which the system said the collateral is going. Nothing in it was looking.

This is structural, not one team's bug. A mint contract on chain B cannot see chain A, so it asks someone. Proof-of-reserve systems answer that question honestly — but the answer is still a report: a number, from a party you must trust, about a moment already past. It says nothing about money already promised to someone else, nothing about supply on other chains, and nothing about what happened between reports.

03
The difference, in one picture

A vault holds $1,000,000. Against it, $600,000 of tokens are issued.

The owner announces they are taking $300,000 out. The money has not moved yet — but it is spoken for.

An ordinary reserve check
$400,000
more tokens may be issued

“Everything is fine.”
It can still see $1,000,000 in the vault, so it still counts all of it.

MintBound
$100,000
more tokens may be issued

“$300,000 of that is leaving.”
It stopped counting the moment the exit was announced.

The $300,000 gap between those two numbers is exactly the withdrawal. Anyone trusting the left-hand figure issues tokens against money already on its way out the door. That is how holders discover a token was backed by nothing — not through fraud, but through arithmetic nobody was doing.

04
What it is

A lock on the mint function that only a proof can open.

MintBound holds mint authority over a wrapped asset and refuses to use it unless Creditcoin's Block Prover precompile has cryptographically verified — inside the minting transaction — that the backing is there.

Not an oracle report. Not a monthly attestation. A Merkle inclusion proof of a specific source-chain transaction, checked by native code, per mint.

It is infrastructure, not an application: the primitive other RWA products on Creditcoin can sit on so the assets they represent cannot quietly become unbacked liabilities.

05
Attestcoin integration — six mechanisms, one invariant

Depth, not a checkpoint.

Remove either precompile and there is no product. Attestcoin is the guard's only source of truth about the source chain.

01 · THE ENABLING TRICK

State-to-event lifting

The prover proves transactions; solvency is a question about state. A permissionless call makes the vault emit its own balance into a log — so a balance becomes a fact about a transaction, and therefore provable. This turns a transaction oracle into a state oracle for any dApp on Creditcoin.

02 · PRECOMPILE 0x0FD2

Verified per mint

Merkle and continuity proofs checked in the same transaction that mints. No reporter anywhere in the path — trustedParties() returns 0.

03 · PRECOMPILE 0x0FD3

Freshness read on-chain

The contract asks Creditcoin itself how stale the proof is. No off-chain party can lie about it, because no off-chain party is asked.

04 · THE WINDOW

Announced exits stop counting

Withdrawals must be announced and are timelocked. An announced exit leaves the backing immediately, not on execution. The delay is required to exceed 2× measured detection latency, enforced at construction.

05 · BOTH SIDES

Liabilities across every chain

Remote wrapped supply is proven the same way and summed. A registered chain that goes quiet freezes minting — unknown liability is never treated as zero.

06 · BETWEEN PROOFS

Continuity under bond

“No outflow over this interval” is expensive to prove and cheap to refute — one inclusion proof does it. So it is asserted under bond, and the bond pays the challenger.

Attestcoin secures itself with “independent verifiers, called Attestors, who each confirm what's true and put real money on the line.” Mechanism 06 applies that same economic pattern one layer up: where the protocol bonds attestors to secure facts, we bond claimants to secure the intervals between facts. We did not merely call the protocol — we extended its own security model to the gap it does not cover.

06
The rule everything serves

Total liabilities across all chains, plus this mint, must not exceed the proven reserve minus announced exits, after haircut.

Two properties follow, and they are the actual specification

  • No mint may ever leave supply above the proven ceiling.
  • If supply is above the ceiling for any reason, minting is already frozen.

The tidier single-statement version — supply ≤ ceiling at all times — is false, and our own invariant fuzzer proved it false against an earlier draft of this specification. A legitimate reserve decrease after minting is a counterexample. We restated the claim rather than weakening the system to fit it.

Underneath sits one asymmetry: increasing liabilities requires a fresh proof; decreasing them requires nothing. Every failure mode — stale proof, silent chain, absent worker, unreachable prover — lands on the same side. Minting stops. Redeeming never does.

The overwhelming majority prove an event happened — a payment settled, a loan was repaid, a delivery occurred — then act on it. That is what the Block Prover is built for, and the natural first thing to build on it.

MintBound proves state, and enforces a balance-sheet invariant on what it proves. Solvency is a question about a balance, not an event — and answering it required making a balance provable in the first place.

This is a claim about category, not about the quality of other work. The RWA track asks for projects that bridge off-chain value with on-chain transparency; reserve solvency is that question, stated exactly.

12
Adoption

It speaks the incumbent's language.

ProvenReserveFeed implements Chainlink's AggregatorV3Interface. Change one address in an existing proof-of-reserve integration and nothing else has to be rewritten.

We are not claiming to be a better Chainlink — Secure Mint already gates minting on proof of reserve, and does the aggregate check correctly. We are a drop-in cryptographic evidence backend for the interface the industry already uses. The repository ships Chainlink's own Secure Mint pattern, written to their published spec with no knowledge of MintBound, running unmodified against our feed in the test suite.

We also publish something no other reserve system does: trustedParties(asset) — 0 for a cryptographically proven reserve, 1 for an oracle-reported one. An integrating protocol can read how much trust its own evidence requires.

The rules ask for cross-chain logic “without relying on centralized oracle operators.” Most projects answer that sentence with a paragraph. We answer it with a number the contract publishes: trustedParties() == 0.

13
Why this belongs in the ecosystem

Auditability as the product, not the afterthought.

Where a local auditor's signature carries little weight and cross-border recourse is theoretical, a monthly attestation is not assurance. Cryptographic proof is the only form of assurance that travels unchanged — identical for a lender in Lagos and a lender in London.

Creditcoin's own mission is credit where institutions are weakest. Every RWA product built on it inherits one question it cannot answer for itself: is the thing backing this still there? MintBound answers that question in the only way that does not require trusting the person being asked — and it does so through the Attestcoin Protocol specifically, because until a native precompile made verification cost 382,578 gas, asking per mint was economically absurd.

Increasing liabilities requires a fresh proof. Decreasing them requires nothing. Everything else is a consequence.
